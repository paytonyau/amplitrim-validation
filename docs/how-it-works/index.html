How AmpliTrim works

It computes what it can compute exactly, and measures what it cannot.

This page shows the design one step at a time. The pictures are schematic: they explain the idea and are not drawn from your data.

The idea

DADA2 does three things to your reads, in this order. AmpliTrim treats each one differently.

  • Replicated exactly

    Filter and trim

    AmpliTrim applies the same rules as filterAndTrim: cut each read at the chosen length, drop reads that have a base of quality 2 or below, or any N, in the part you keep, and drop reads over the expected-error limit (maxEE). The share that passes is computed, not guessed.

  • Not modelled

    Denoise

    Denoising (dada) is DADA2's most expensive step, and a quick test cannot reproduce it. This is why AmpliTrim's yield is an estimate, and an optimistic one.

  • Measured on raw reads

    Merge pairs

    DADA2's mergePairs works on denoised reads. AmpliTrim merges your trimmed reads with vsearch instead, at DADA2's minimum overlap of 12, so you see how many pairs really merge.

What the cut does

A forward and a reverse read each start at one end of the amplicon, the stretch of DNA that was amplified. truncLenF and truncLenR say where to cut each read. Together they have to be at least as long as the amplicon plus 12, so that the reads overlap, and no longer than the quality allows.

forward readreverse readoverlapnoisy end

  • Too short

    The reads stop before they meet. There is a gap, so they cannot merge.

  • Just enough

    The reads overlap by at least 12 nt, DADA2's minimum. They merge.

  • Too long

    They overlap, but the last bases of each read are noisy. More reads fail the quality filter, and you lose them.

AmpliTrim looks for the shortest cut that still lets your longest amplicons merge, and splits it between the two reads where the quality allows.

Step by step

AmpliTrim runs these steps on a sample of your reads.

  1. 1Take a sample of your reads

    AmpliTrim takes every Nth read pair across all your samples, up to 50,000 pairs, and uses the same pairs for everything below. The sample is the same every time you run it.

    In the code: build_subsample

  2. 2Measure your amplicons

    It merges the full-length reads with vsearch. The lengths of the merged reads are your amplicon lengths, so you do not need to look them up.

    Only pairs that overlap at full length can be seen. If fewer than 100 pairs do, the run stops with a message that says so.

    In the code: estimate_insert_sizes

  3. 3Set the floor

    AmpliTrim leaves out the longest 0.2% of the amplicons and adds 12, DADA2's minimum overlap. The result is the shortest total cut length that can merge the rest, and AmpliTrim never tests anything shorter.

    In the code: coverage_floor

  4. 4Try cuts above the floor

    From the floor up to the full read length, in steps of 2 nt, AmpliTrim asks the quality table for the best split between the forward and reverse reads. That table holds the share of pairs that pass DADA2's filter at every cut.

    It then filters the reads at that cut and merges them with vsearch. The share that passes times the share that merges is the yield. The search stops when a longer cut gains less than 1 point.

    In the code: _search, pass_table, run_vsearch

  5. 5Pick the cut

    Cuts whose yield is within 1 point of the best are treated as tied. Among them, AmpliTrim picks the one with the least overlap, which means the shortest reads and the fewest noisy bases. A cut that cannot merge your longest amplicons is never picked.

    In the code: select_recommendation

  6. 6Set maxEE

    At the chosen cut, for each read, AmpliTrim finds the limit that covers three quarters of the reads: the 75th percentile of their expected errors, plus 1. It is never below 2, DADA2's default. There is no upper limit: the report notes it when a limit goes above 5.

    If the limit is not the default for both reads, the search and the pick are repeated with it.

    In the code: _rule_max_ee

  7. 7Write the report

    You get REPORT.md (the settings, copy-paste snippets for DADA2 and QIIME 2, what to expect, and warnings), summary.json (the same result for scripts) and summary.png (a figure).

    In the code: write_outputs

  8. +With --its

    ITS amplicons vary a lot in length, so DADA2's ITS workflow does not truncate. AmpliTrim evaluates exactly that: each read is cut at its first base of quality 2 or below, then kept if it is long enough, free of N and within maxEE.

    The floor, the search and the maxEE rule are skipped. The report shows where the pairs go instead.

    In the code: _run_its

Why these choices

Every choice is listed with how well it held up in our tests. Some are design choices with no test behind them, and we say which.

ChoiceWhyHow it held up
Replicate DADA2's quality filterThe share of pairs that pass is then computed, not guessed.Held

The predicted pass rate was within 0.8 points of DADA2's in every test dataset.

Measure merging with real merges, at DADA2's minimum overlap of 12Merging depends on the real overlaps and mismatches in your reads, which arithmetic cannot know.Held

With these cuts, DADA2 merged as many pairs as with the other tool's cuts: a tie.

Find the amplicon length from your readsYou do not have to look it up, and it is right for your data.Design choice

Only pairs that overlap at full length are seen, so the longest amplicons are under-represented.

Use a sample of at most 50,000 pairsThe run time and the memory use stay bounded, however large your dataset is.Not yet timed

The bound is by design. We have not yet timed the released version.

Count merges with a tolerant setting for the yieldA strict count tracked DADA2 less well in our tests.Optimistic

The yield is an estimate made before DADA2 denoises the reads. Use it to compare settings, and run DADA2 once to confirm.

Break ties by the least overlapShorter reads mean fewer noisy bases.Design choice

Cuts within the tolerance of the best are equivalent on yield, so this is a matter of preference.

Set maxEE to cover three quarters of the reads, never below 2Reads with noisy ends fail DADA2's default limit, and a looser limit can recover them.Sensible default, not independently tested

It follows DADA2's author: keep maxEE 2 unless your reads need more, and relax it somewhat when they do. The 75th percentile is our own choice, made after seeing the validation data (the author gives no ceiling, so the rule has none; the report notes a limit above 5), and the rule did not reach the result we had decided in advance would make us adopt it. We keep it as a trade-off that we state openly.

Offer few optionsThree required arguments, --its and --verbose leave little room to misuse it.Design choice

Everything else is chosen for you, including maxEE.

The full results, including what did not hold, are on the validation page. The user guide explains how maxEE is chosen.

What AmpliTrim does not do

  • It does not denoise. The yield is measured before DADA2 denoises the reads, so DADA2 can keep fewer pairs.
  • It checks quality and mergeability, not accuracy.
  • It does not recommend settings for other denoisers, such as Deblur.
  • It cannot help when your reads do not overlap. If your amplicons are longer than your two reads can span, no cut can merge them.

The package is eight modules. The last column shows which others each one uses.
