How well does AmpliTrim work?

Test results on 12 public datasets, one page each.

AmpliTrim chooses DADA2 settings for you. To find out how good its choices are, we tested it against real DADA2 runs on 12 public datasets: mock communities, real samples and one ITS case study. We wrote down what we expected before the runs. These pages show what held and what did not.

How to read these pages

Estimate and measurement
AmpliTrim estimates how many read pairs will pass DADA2's filter and merge. We then ran DADA2 and measured it. The pages compare the two.
Points
Differences are in percentage points of the read pairs that went in. A difference of +2 means 2 more pairs in every 100.
maxEE
The most expected errors DADA2 allows in a read. Reads over the limit are dropped. DADA2's default is 2.
A cut
Where each read is cut: 247/199 means the forward reads at 247 nt and the reverse reads at 199 nt.
The other tool
An existing automatic tool for choosing truncation lengths. We compare AmpliTrim with it; the findings name it.
The shaded band
In the charts, the margin we set in advance. A result inside the band counts as equivalent.
Colours
Blue: more than the comparison. Orange: fewer. Marks drawn in a single colour are plain measurements.
Our datasets
9 datasets were kept aside and not used in the design (independent tests). 2 were used while designing AmpliTrim (Mock community A and Soil, V4), so they are shown but are not independent evidence. One is the ITS case study.
Rounds of tests
We tested in three rounds. The first round called two cuts equivalent if they merged within 1 point of each other; later rounds used 2 points, which is what these pages show. A dataset's page says when the stricter margin changes the picture.

What held

What we testedWhat happened
AmpliTrim's estimate of the share of pairs that pass DADA2's filterHeld

On all 12 datasets the estimate was within 0.8 points of what DADA2 measured. The target we set before testing was 2 points. The average miss was 0.2 points.

AmpliTrim's estimate of the share of pairs that mergePartly

On 9 of the 11 datasets where the order could be compared, it put the settings in exactly the order DADA2 did, and it did less well on Mock community A (0.90) and Wastewater, V3-V4 (0.80). The estimate is also off in size, because it stops before DADA2 denoises the reads: from 2.2 points too low to 32.1 points too high, depending on the dataset. Use it to compare settings, not as a forecast.

AmpliTrim's cut against the other tool's, at the same maxEEHeld

AmpliTrim's cut merged more pairs on 4 of 10 datasets, the same on 5 and fewer on 1 (average +0.4 points). Only one dataset differs by more than the 2-point margin we set, so the cuts are equivalent, not better.

Getting the same cut on other samplesPartly

Run on three separate sets of samples, AmpliTrim chose the same cut on 4 of 6 datasets and the other tool on 3 of 6. AmpliTrim's misses are small moves along a flat stretch where the yield barely changes.

The maxEE ruleSensible default, not independently tested

It follows the advice of DADA2's author: maxEE 2 is a good general-purpose value, and relaxing it somewhat is also OK. The rule keeps 2 unless your reads need more. The numbers are ours: we chose the 75th percentile after seeing our test data, and the author gives no ceiling, so the rule has none; the report notes a limit above 5. So it is a sensible default that has not been tested on independent data, and it did not reach the result we had decided in advance would make us adopt it (that test used an earlier version of the rule, with a cap of 5, which merged 60.7% on average). On the five datasets where it differs from the default, DADA2 merged 45.0% of the pairs on average with the default maxEE, 61.2% with maxEE 2,5 and 64.9% with the released rule, measured afterwards. We did not apply the criteria again to the new numbers, because we had seen them first. We keep the rule and say so openly.

The full list of criteria and outcomes, written before the runs, is in the test register.

All datasets at a glance

How close was the pass-rate estimate?

AmpliTrim's cut against the other tool's, at the same maxEE

* Wastewater is the only real dataset in these tests with poor read quality: its reads are noisy (an estimated 54% of its pairs pass DADA2's default filter), and the loss is to read quality, not to short reads. Its reads were also trimmed to mixed lengths, and every pair was in reverse orientation. It is the one case where the other tool was ahead. Its page explains why.

By group: real samples (4 datasets): ahead on 2, tied on 2 (average +0.6 points). Mock communities (5): ahead on 2, tied on 3 (average +0.6 points). Wastewater (1): behind by 1.0 point.
