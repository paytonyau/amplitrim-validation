# AmpliTrim: future development

Status of 12 October 2026. **Nothing on this list is implemented, and none of it is part of 0.1.0.** Each item says why it is open and what it needs. Anything that would change a recommendation needs a test whose criteria are written in `TEST_REGISTER.md` before it is run, as in nights 1 to 5.

## Before and at the release (logistics, not development)

- Build and upload to TestPyPI, check how the PyPI page renders, then publish to PyPI.
- Switch on Zenodo, tag `v0.1.0`, and put the DOI and the release date in `CITATION.cff`.
- Write the Bioconda recipe (it needs the checksum of the PyPI archive); after the release, add `pip install amplitrim` and the conda command to the README quick start, and a release badge.
- Finish the JOSS paper (`paper/`): fill the three author markers, verify every reference, and submit after the release DOI exists. At submission the paper, its workflow and its test move back to the software repository.
- Fill the seven "source study: to add" entries of the results site, and add the real AmpliTrim report of each dataset (run `validation/collect_results.py`, then rebuild the site).
- Upload the social preview image on GitHub (Settings, Social preview). The brand kit is still in the first colours; regenerate it if the new theme stays.
- Re-check against the primary pages what is still marked "not re-checked" in `docs/FINDINGS.md`: the DADA2 tutorial's advice on relaxing to (2,5), the overlap rule, and the mention of FIGARO as an alternative.

## Algorithm and validation

1. **A simpler cut.** Take the shortest total that covers the amplicons (the longest minus 0.2%, plus the minimum overlap), then the exact best split, and drop the search over longer totals, the yield estimate and the four-key tie-break. Evidence so far (`TEST_REGISTER.md`, section 24): the floor-only cut merged as many pairs as the released cut on all four datasets where the two differ, and 2.3 points more on wastewater (9 of 9 samples). But the pre-set test of equality (P43) was not met (7 of 11), so this needs a new test with its own criteria: all 12 datasets through DADA2 at the floor-only cut, and the stability of the pick over three sample sets.
2. **The `maxEE` rule.** It is a sensible default that has not been tested on independent data.
   - Explain why inter-laboratory V4-V6 was run by night 4b at `maxEE` 3 and is recommended by the released package at 4,3: one unit of forward limit is worth 5.5 points there, and the rule's limit is a step function of a percentile.
   - Validate on new datasets, including rich communities, because what noisier reads do to relative abundances is not measured.
   - Consider a limit expressed as expected errors per 100 nt, which a fixed number cannot be for reads of different lengths.
   - Test another percentile only on data that were not used to choose 0.75.
3. **Repeat the comparison with the other tool for the released package.** The README's comparison (same `maxEE`, the other tool's cut against AmpliTrim's) was run with the development build; the search code is the same and picked the same cuts on ten datasets, but the picks at the other tool's `maxEE` were not compared.
4. **A better estimate.** The estimate stops before DADA2 denoises the reads and is optimistic (on wastewater it preferred the cut that DADA2 liked less). Ideas considered: model the loss in denoising, a strict merge as the headline, an intermediate vsearch cutoff.

## Features

5. **A long-read mode** (PacBio HiFi full-length 16S first, then the whole rRNA operon, 16S-ITS-23S). No truncation and no merging; `maxEE`, `minLen` and `maxLen` from the length distribution of your reads. It needs a single-read input, its own rule (DADA2's author says that keeping about 40% of the reads is fine and that more low-quality reads may not help, so a rule that targets 75% retention does not carry over), and datasets with expected sequences. Nanopore is not claimed: DADA2's author says it works fine with HiFi reads.
6. **A long-amplicon tail diagnostic in the report.** On D6300 V4-V5 about 1% of the amplicons are 550 nt against a median of 374, and leaving out 1% instead of 0.2% raised the merged share from 57.2% to 89.8%. A report line saying what leaving out 1% would allow, and the coverage setting as an option, would let users decide.
7. **Smaller ideas, each set aside for 0.1.0:** a `maxEE` adjustment for ITS; a forward-heavier split; a minimum-overlap sweep; a per-cell DADA2 flow; Deblur support (it takes a trim length only); a running-time comparison on the released build (not yet timed).

## Long-read amplicon mode: design notes (12 October 2026)

From the discussion; nothing is built, and platform facts are from memory unless a source is named. Treat every parameter below as a hypothesis to test.

**Scope.** Any marker, and read structures rather than companies. Short paired reads are today's case (Illumina; Element and MGI would need their quality scores checked). A new **single-read** structure would cover PacBio HiFi first, and Nanopore or Ion Torrent only if DADA2's position on them is established from its own pages. Each platform gets a small profile: read structure, quality-score behaviour, suggested error model, default length bounds, and the source of its guidance.

**The protocol (any long-read amplicon, any platform).** The backbone is the one we use for short reads; the middle changes.

*Run card* (stated by the user, or detected and flagged): platform, chemistry, basecaller or CCS version; library type (a single amplicon, several multiplexed loci, concatemers such as Kinnex, UMI-tagged); barcodes; both primers; the expected amplicon (length range, number of expected variants, coding or not); what the provider already did (demultiplexed, primers or adapters removed, re-oriented, quality-trimmed, binned qualities); the goal (exact variants, clusters, consensus, taxonomy).

1. **Provenance.** Checksums, tool versions and the run card are recorded with the data.
2. **Long-read QC.** Read count; length histogram on a log scale and zoomed on the expected length; quality distribution; per-position quality at both ends against the middle; signs of concatemers or chimeras (reads near a multiple of the expected length, internal primers).
3. **Demultiplex and remove adapters** where the files are not already split (barcodes may sit at both ends; check that the pair of barcodes is valid).
4. **Primers and orientation.** Match both primers anchored at the two ends, on both strands, with more tolerance for mismatches than for Illumina; orient every read to one strand. The share of reads with both primers, one, none, or an internal primer is itself a quality metric, and is reported. Reads with both primers have their ends fixed at the amplicon boundaries, so **no end truncation follows**.
5. **Split concatemers and flag chimeras** before any length or error decision.
6. **Length model.** Measure the distribution on the primer-trimmed reads; find its modes; compare with the expected amplicon (from the user, or an in-silico PCR); set one window for each expected amplicon (several for multiplexed loci); report the reads outside each window, with counts, lengths and the probable cause.
7. **Error model and `maxEE`.** Expected errors per read and per 1,000 nt; retention at a grid of `maxEE`; the platform's standard against what the data say. Quality scores do not mean the same on every platform, so each platform profile states how they are used. For a coding amplicon, add a check of how many reads keep the reading frame, since indels in homopolymers shift it.
8. **Filter** by the chosen `minLen`, `maxLen` and `maxEE` (and N), with a read-loss table for every step.
9. **Denoise or cluster, a branch that depends on the goal and on evidence.** Exact variants only where an error model is documented and validated for the platform (DADA2 with PacBio HiFi); identity-threshold clusters, a consensus per sample or per UMI, or classification alone where it is not. AmpliTrim should say which branch the evidence supports and not choose it silently.
10. **Chimera removal, then annotation.** Check that the reference database suits long queries: Buetas et al. note that databases were not prepared for full-length 16S queries.
11. **Controls and report.** A mock community and negative controls go through every step identically. The report lists the parameters, the standard used, what the data said, the read loss, and the warnings. Evaluate by variants recovered, extra variants and reads in exact variants, not by abundances.

**What differs, and what does not.** Steps 1 to 8 are the same for every long-read amplicon. What differs is: the **platform profile** (quality-score meaning, error types, default length bounds, suggested error model, source of the guidance); the **marker profile** (expected length and its spread, whether several peaks are expected, copy number and variation within a genome, coding or not); and the **goal** (variants, clusters or consensus), which decides step 9. For ITS, DADA2's workflow uses `truncQ` 2, which comes from Illumina; whether it means anything for PacBio or Nanopore qualities is not established and may do harm.

**Where AmpliTrim fits.** It is the advisor for steps 2, 6, 7 and 8, and a reporter for step 4. It does not demultiplex, remove primers, denoise or cluster: those stay with specialised tools, as cutadapt and DADA2 do today. Its input would be the reads (best after primer removal) and, optionally, the run card; where the run card is missing, it detects what it can and flags what it assumed.

**What already exists.** `validation/longread_describe.py` measures steps 2, 4, 6 and 7 for any single-end FASTQ files and any pair of primers: the share of reads with both, one or no primer and the share in the reverse orientation; the length between the primers (percentiles, the commonest bin, the share within 10% of the median, the shares much longer or shorter); expected errors per read and per 1,000 nt; the share kept at each `maxEE`; the number of distinct quality values; and the quality of the first 50 bases, the middle half and the last 50 bases. It makes no decision and uses mismatches only (no indels) for the primers. Night 6 runs it on 26 PacBio runs.

**What the first real HiFi data showed (Night 6, 26 files, `TEST_REGISTER.md` section 26).** (1) A mismatch-only primer match missed one of the two primers in 16.3% of the reads and both in 0.7%, the pattern that independent misses at 8.9% per primer give, and the share with both primers followed the quality of the file (rank correlation +0.89). **cutadapt, with indels allowed, then found both primers in 99.9% and 99.7% of the reads of two files (75% and 93% before): the primers were there, with an insertion or a deletion in 8% to 26% of them.** The describer now matches by edit distance and reports each primer's detection rate; any long-read mode must do the same, because "the share with both primers" by a mismatch-only match hides the problem, and reads whose primer is missed are the noisier ones, so every figure computed on the rest is optimistic. (2) Among the reads with both primers the expected errors have a hard ceiling near 15 (1% of 1,454 nt, the HiFi definition), the median is close to zero in the good files, and a tail of 10% to 20% of the reads carries most of the errors: retention was 77.9% at `maxEE` 2 (56% to 88% over the files), 89.0% at 5 and 96.5% at 10. A fixed `maxEE` therefore keeps very different shares of different runs of the same kit (the six `-R` runs against the other 20), which supports a per-run measurement and a length-aware standard rather than one number. (3) The Night 6 DADA2 run was the paired short-read workflow on the Illumina half only; nothing here ran DADA2 on the PacBio reads. DADA2's own primer removal, as I remember its defaults (up to 2 mismatches, no indels; not verified here), would discard reads that carry an indel in a primer, which was 8% to 26% of the reads in the two files checked. The loss that Buetas et al. report for PacBio between the reads sequenced and the reads kept (faeces 12,702 to 7,350; saliva 21,151 to 17,444) also contains quality filtering and chimera removal, so it is not evidence of this; `removePrimers` on SRR23380883 would show it. (4) The quality at the 3' end was lower than the middle by 2.5 Q pooled and by more than 3 Q in 5 files, all in the lower-quality runs: end quality has to be measured per run, as planned, and not assumed uniform.

**What PacBio's own application brief says ("Microbiome and metagenome sequencing with HiFi reads", revision 10, 5 August 2026, pasted by the researcher on 14 October 2026).** It lists DADA2 first among the routes for analysing full-length 16S data (with QIIME 2, microbiomehelper, OneCodex, EZBiome, seqSight and PacBio's own GitHub pipeline, `HiFi-16S-workflow`), which supports a long-read mode for a tool that sets DADA2's parameters. It gives the current systems as Vega and Revio with SPRQ-Nx chemistry and typical depths per sample of about 15,000 reads (Vega) and 20,000 (Revio) for standard 16S, and about 30,000 and 45,000 for Kinnex 16S; Sequel II, the platform of the Buetas data and of the ATCC MSA-1003 dataset, is the previous generation. A long-read mode therefore needs a platform profile for each chemistry, and development data from Revio or Vega SPRQ-Nx besides Sequel II. The brief names no dataset links; its only addresses are the GitHub pipelines and pacb.com/microbial-pop.

**The length window (`minLen`, `maxLen`) between the primers: what the data say (14 October 2026).** Monomer mock, three files: median 1,466 to 1,474 nt; first to 99th percentile 1,389 to 1,493 nt; 99.95% to 99.99% of the reads within 10% of the median (about 1,319 to 1,621 nt); shortest 868 nt, longest 1,978 to 2,739 nt. Buetas (first run): median 1,454 nt; first to 99th percentile about 1,421 to 1,513 nt pooled (files: first percentile 1,392 to 1,433, 99th percentile 1,482 to 1,622); 99.6% within 10% of the median (about 1,309 to 1,599 nt); shortest 277 nt, longest 3,676 nt; 0.06% over 1.5 times the median. A window of 10% either side of the median therefore costs at most 0.4% of the reads, in a mock and in human samples alike, so the choice inside any reasonable window matters little for retention; the window is there for specificity (concatemers and fragments). **Proposed rule, not validated:** `minLen` = 0.9 times and `maxLen` = 1.1 times the median length between the primers, rounded to 10 nt, which gives about 1,300 and 1,600 nt for both datasets; the median is taken over the run, not over one sample, because the Kinnex demultiplexing report suggests that some samples may have a shorter amplicon (to be read from the Kinnex describer table); the lengths are those after primer removal, so without it 39 nt (20 for 27F and 19 for 1492R) are added; a marker whose length varies legitimately, such as ITS, would need a window based on percentiles instead. **To test:** that the window keeps every expected strain of the mock (the lengths of the expected amplicons from the 20 genomes, with `make_reference.py`) and at least 99.5% of the reads of every Monomer file; the describer could also report the share kept by a given window (a small addition, not made).

**Proposed long-read tests, with their gates (written 14 October 2026, before any of them was run; to be moved to the register as section 27 when their scripts exist; the runs are numbered 15 (L1, the description of all 96 Monomer files), 16 (L2, the Kinnex files) and 17 (Night 7: L3, L4, the genomes and the expected amplicons)).** *L1, the length window on all 96 Monomer files* (needs the 96 files, 2.86 GB, and the existing describer): the gate is that in every file at least 99.5% of the reads are within 10% of the file's median length (`share_within_10pct`, a proxy for a run-wide window) and that the largest file median is at most 1.05 times the smallest, so that one window serves the run. *L2, Kinnex against Monomer on the same three barcode pairs* (needs the three Kinnex files and the existing describer): (a) the share of reads at or under `maxEE` 2 is lower in the Kinnex reads than in the Monomer reads in each pair, by at least 2 points; (b) it is at least 62% in each Kinnex file (the lower bound taken from the report); (c) the share at or under `maxEE` 15, about 1% of the length, is at least 0.5 points lower in the Kinnex reads than in the Monomer reads in each pair (the ceiling of the HiFi filter does not hold for the segments of a long read). If (a) fails the two library types may share one standard. *L3, DADA2's own primer removal against the describer* (descriptive, no gate; `removePrimers` with its defaults on one Monomer file, to be compared with 99.0% of the reads having both primers with indels allowed). *L4, agreement of the filter estimate with DADA2* (needs a new script: cutadapt removes both primers, allowing indels and reverse-complementing the reads that came in the reverse orientation, then DADA2's `filterAndTrim` with only `maxEE` acting, that is `truncQ` 0, `minQ` 0, `maxN` 0, no length limits and `rm.phix` off): for `maxEE` 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 the share kept by DADA2 is within 2 points of the describer's estimate in every file, on the three Monomer files, the three Kinnex files and three Buetas files; if it fails, the cause (the boundaries of the primers, reads with one primer only) is found before anything else is done. *L5, consistency across replicates* (descriptive, to be designed): the 96 Monomer samples are probably replicates of one mock, so the variants found in only one or two replicates are likely errors, which gives a measure of spurious variants without a reference; the number of variants and the share present in at least 90% of the replicates, for each `maxEE` and window, with the PacBio error model of DADA2. *Tests that need the strains (truth)*: T1 every expected strain lies inside the window; T2 the share of expected variants recovered at each `maxEE`; T3 extra variants; T4 chimeras; T5 the accuracy of the abundances in the staggered mix.

**Outcome of L3 (14 October 2026, descriptive): DADA2's `removePrimers` with its defaults, on the Monomer file Fwd_07--Rev_30.** It wrote 20,696 of 24,730 reads (83.7%), against 99.0% of the reads (24,470) with both primers when the describer allows insertions and deletions: 15.3 points, 3,774 reads, fewer. **What the parameters would be based on.**
- `minLen` and `maxLen`: the window comes from the reads (main peak or peaks, margin to be tested). The natural length distribution for the primer pair (in-silico PCR on a curated database: percentiles 0.1, 1, 5, 50, 95, 99, 99.9, mean, median, mode, peaks, by group) is a plausibility prior that flags data, not a rule. The extremes of a database are dominated by oddities. For the whole operon the ITS length varies, so use complete genomes.
- `maxEE`: expected errors grow with read length (at Q30, 1,450 nt carries about 1.45 and 4,500 nt about 4.5), so a fixed number is not length-neutral. Stage A: report only. Stage B: a standard expressed per 1,000 nt, scaled to the read length. Stage C: a data-driven default, only after validation with criteria written first. Our short-read rule (about 75% retention) does not carry over, because DADA2's author says keeping about 40% of long reads is fine.
